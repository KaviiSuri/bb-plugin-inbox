## Notes per product

**T3 Code** is the closest relative. ## Where prior art argues against a decided point

1. **"Clear = verified sign-off."** No product here records a verification. Every clear is a dismissal (Done, archive, settle, mark read), and T3 even **auto-settles** on PR merge or inactivity. The only verification-like gate is Conductor's: merge when checks are green and comments and todos are handled. Risk: if the only way to clear is a sign-off, people may rubber-stamp it, or the queue may rot. Worth prototyping a cheap "seen" or snooze next to "verify". Every product has one.
2. **"`human`-audience child gets its own item."** T3 does the opposite: subagents never get a row, and their request (`approval` / `user_input`) shows on the **parent's** row (T3 `packages/client-runtime/src/state/models.ts:162, 256`). Both are defensible. The LOGIC prototype should be able to toggle between them.
3. **Tier 1 as a single tier.** Plannotator splits "blocked on you" three ways (stopped / holding up work / other question), using urgency the agent declares, and puts "waiting on the agent" (Sent) *below* "new to you". The rest of our order (blocked > broken > done-unseen > running) matches T3's folder-dot precedence.

## Ideas for our prototypes

**UI: five structurally different Inbox layouts**

- **A. Tier lanes plus a pinned-bottom shelf.** Collapsible sections per tier, newest first. A footer row reads "Running 4" (T3 `mt-auto` shelves, Vibe Kanban's three sections). A folded section shows a bold count when something new arrives (Plannotator).
- **B. Flat ranked list, evidence on hover or expand.** One list sorted by tier then recency, with a tier glyph per row. Expanding a row in place shows the diff stat, tests, PR checks and child summaries (Linear and GitHub, with a preview inside the row because the sidebar is narrow).
- **C. One-at-a-time focus card.** Superhuman-style: one card with its evidence and Verify / Later / Open buttons, then an "Inbox zero" end screen. Later items come back after the rest (Plannotator Decision mode).
- **D. Two tabs: "Needs you" and "Everything".** "Needs you" holds tiers 1-2; "Everything" adds Verify, FYI and Running (Linear Priority vs Other, Agent Inbox's Interrupted default).
- **E. Tree with attention badges.** No separate list. The Threads tree gets an "attention only" filter, and each collapsed group shows its most urgent descendant as a dot (T3 folder dot). The cheapest variant, and a useful control.

**Logic: copy or reject**

- *Copy.* A verified item comes back on new work, keeping its verification watermark. T3 un-settles on any new message, and the whole thread comes back. Plannotator's version is better: it comes back only if something still waits, returns to the archive by itself once handled, and the thread shows a "New since you looked" divider from a per-person seen watermark (`packages/inbox/snoozeBack.ts`, `seq` vs `person_seen_seq` in `inbox-types.ts`). That's one candidate answer to our open question: store `verifiedAt` plus the evidence baseline (diff base sha, PR head), and re-surface scoped to what changed since then.
- *Copy.* Don't re-surface while the thread is running again. Bring it back on finish, question or failure. That's T3's snooze rule: only a new question or a fresh failure wakes early, never a completion.
- *Copy.* Exit grace for running → done (T3's 2 s), skipped when the thread turns actionable. This stops a thread that pauses between runs from flickering in and out of the inbox.
- *Copy.* Freeze the order while the user is looking at the list or an item, and re-sort when they leave (Plannotator settle). With "one item per thread, kept current", rows will otherwise jump under the cursor.
- *Copy.* Running collapses to a counted, collapsible shelf whose open state persists (T3, Vibe Kanban). Running rows keep "Running" even when unseen (Vibe Kanban).
- *Reject or flag.* Auto-clear on PR merge (T3 auto-settle) contradicts clear = verified. At most, a merge with green checks could become evidence that makes Verify one click.
- *Consider.* "Do I owe a reply?" as an AI label (Inbox Zero To Reply / Awaiting Reply) matches our planned cheap-model check. Plannotator gets the same signal structurally, from agent-declared `Stopped:` / `Holds up:` fields. A spawn-time or tool-level declaration could replace the model call.
